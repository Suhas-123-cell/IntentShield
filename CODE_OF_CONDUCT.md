# Code of conduct

This is a small open-source project, and the aim is simple: people can ask questions, report problems and propose
changes without being treated badly.

- Be respectful. Disagree with the code, the numbers or the design as hard as you like; do not make it personal.
- No harassment, insults, discrimination or unwelcome attention, in issues, pull requests, discussions or anywhere
  else the project is the subject.
- Assume good faith, say plainly what you do not understand, and keep criticism specific and checkable. A report that
  an evaluation claim is overstated is welcome; the project's own notes ask for it.
- Do not post other people's private information, credentials or working attacks against a third-party product.
  `SECURITY.md` says how to report a security problem.

## Reporting a problem

- **In public:** open an issue on the repository. Describe what happened and link it; do not name anyone you do not need to.
- **In private:** use GitHub's private reporting form on the repository (the Security tab, "Report a vulnerability"), the
  route [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) gives for sensitive reports. The form is meant for security problems, so put "Conduct
  report" at the start of the title; the report is visible to the repository's maintainers and not to other users. There is
  no separate conduct email address.

The maintainer decides what to do and may edit or remove comments, close threads and block accounts that break these rules. The project has one maintainer, so a report about the maintainer has no independent reviewer; GitHub's own abuse reporting is the route for that case.

This applies to everyone who takes part in the project, the maintainer included.
